Correction to version 1

Two things were wrong in version 1. It looked at one week, which was too small a window — Need Love came out on top there and is actually fourth over a month. And it forced every report into a single category, so whichever keyword list ran first won the report; running it again in a different order gave a different ranking. Both are fixed. A report is now counted under every theme it touches, which is why the numbers below add up to more than 281.

281 write-ups. 189 different operators. The most-reported person has eight.

That spread is the finding. If a handful of people were driving this, it would be a coaching problem. Spread across 189 operators in 32 days, it is a training problem — and the four themes below say which parts of the training.

The four themes, and what each one needs

Two are missing material. Two are taught already and still going wrong. Those need opposite fixes, and treating them the same way would fix neither.

1

Address, drop-off and pin

Already taught

126 reports — 45% of everything. The pin is the single most-mentioned thing in the whole dataset.

the PIN ...................... 49
drop-off ..................... 20
wrong or incorrect address ... 19
airport or terminal .......... 13

The deck already says to confirm addresses back to the customer, in those words. So this is not people not knowing — it is a step that is not becoming a habit.

One thing worth Oscar's eye: the deck says "confirm the location — don't waste time pin pointing just yet" during the estimate. Sensible for speed. But the pin is now the most reported single item, so it is worth checking whether anything later in the call brings people back to it.

2

Fees, fares and refunds

Barely in the deck

105 reports — 37%. Cancellation fees lead, refunds close behind.

cancellation fee ..... 45
refund ............... 28
fare ................. 14
surge pricing ......... 3
multi-stop wait fee ... 1

The deck covers the $0.27-per-minute concierge fee thoroughly, with the exact words to say. Every other fee a customer can hit — cancellation, surge, wait time — is absent. So is what to do when someone asks for a refund.

3

Membership and plan wording

Taught, and still going wrong

60 reports — 21%. Nearly all of them land on the same moment: the free trial ending and the charge landing.

"we briefly mentioned the $29.99 plan and the 7-day trial, but we did
not clearly explain that after the trial the charge would take place"

"provided the customer with a 7-day trial without explaining our
available plans in detail and advising a 245.88 charge would be
taking place"

The deck does cover plan pricing, with the dollar savings worked out. What the reports keep describing is not a missing number — it is the trial-to-charge handoff being mentioned rather than confirmed. That is a script wording fix, not a new module.

4

Need Love tickets

Weakest quiz module

47 reports — 17%. Filing a duplicate when a ticket already exists is the most common, then picking the wrong category.

tagged Driver Canceled But Took Caller Anyway → should be False Trip
tagged Fare Review → should be False Trip
tagged False Trip → should be Fare Review
filed for Driver Canceled → should be a Fare Review

The corrections point at the same deciding factor every time — the ride status. And separately, this is the module the August class scored worst on.

A step was skipped, not fumbled

36 reports use language like "did not disclose", "failed to explain", "never confirmed". That phrasing separates doing a thing wrong from not doing it at all — and only the second kind gets fixed by changing a script.

A fee, charge, fare or price was not said20
Trial, plan or membership terms were not said18
The address, pin or drop-off was not confirmed7
Accessibility or mobility needs were not checked0

Money is what goes unsaid. Nearly every one of these 36 involves a price, a fee or a plan — a report can be counted in both rows above. That is the argument for a written disclosure line, said the same way every time, the way the concierge fee already is.

The deck is good. The write-ups are around it.

Twenty-seven pages, real scripts, genuinely thorough. It teaches registration well. Most of what gets written up happens around the edges of it.

Covered, and covered well

  • Full call structure, intro through confirmation
  • Concierge fee, disclosed word for word
  • Plan pricing with the dollar savings worked out
  • Confirming addresses back to the customer
  • Mobility and accessibility questions
  • The $1 pre-authorization hold
  • Two recorded calls to listen to

Not in anything I could see

  • Cancellation fees — when they apply, what to say
  • Surge pricing
  • Multi-stop wait fees
  • What to do when someone asks for a refund
  • Which Need Love category to file, and why
  • Checking for an existing ticket first
  • Airport pickup procedure
  • When and how the pin gets set

What I could not see

  • One reviewer files a quarter of everything. Mutya Seno filed 71 of the 281; the next three filed 33, 21 and 18. Report volume partly reflects who is watching, not only who is erring — so an operator with more reports is not automatically worse.
  • Themes overlap on purpose. 94 reports mention two or more, so the numbers add to more than 281. An earlier version forced one theme per report and the ranking changed depending on which keyword list ran first. This one does not have that problem, but it also cannot tell you what a report is "really" about.
  • 40 reports matched no theme at all. 14% of the month is uncategorised. Reading those by hand would probably surface a fifth theme.
  • 124 of 281 reports are cut off. The Slack reader truncates long fields with "...". Theme counts survive it because the giveaway words come early, but many corrections are half-sentences. Getting the full text needs a Slack bot token hitting the API directly.
  • Only four training decks. New material is being written that I have not seen. A Need Love module or a fees module may already exist or be in progress — "not in the deck" here means "not in the four decks I have".
  • Report volume is climbing — 51, 53, 33, 60, then 84 by week. That may be more errors, or more reviewers looking. This data cannot tell which.
  • No call recordings yet. Built entirely on what reviewers wrote, not on what was said. The transcripts exist in the database and would sharpen every line of this.
  • Quiz columns were read off a PDF. Module order comes from the sheet header. Worth confirming Need Love is the ninth column.

What I would do first

  1. Give the pin its own named step.49 mentions — the most of anything in the month. The deck currently defers pinning during the estimate and nothing brings it back.
  2. Write the cancellation fee into the script.45 reports. The deck already handles the concierge fee word-for-word; this needs the same treatment, plus surge and wait fees.
  3. Change "mention the trial" to "confirm the charge".18 reports say the trial-to-charge handoff was mentioned but not confirmed. This is a wording fix to material that already exists.
  4. Add a refund page.28 reports involve refunds and there is nothing in the four decks about handling a refund request.
  5. One page: if the ride status is X, file Y.Every wrong-category correction names the ride status as the deciding factor. Add "check for an existing ticket first" — 19 duplicates.
  6. Make the Need Love quiz practical.Use the real tickets from this month — the right answer is already written in each one. The class's top Need Love scorer still filed one wrong, so recall is not the thing to test.
  7. Rerun this every month, automatically.Operator, team lead and CallLog ID are on every report; 257 of 281 carry a usable call ID. The counting is already code.
