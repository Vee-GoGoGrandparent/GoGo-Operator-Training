Three gaps worth closing

Each one is drawn from the reports themselves, not from an opinion about the training.

Gap 01 — the deciding rule is never taught

False Trip, Cancellation Fee, or Fare Review

The reports show the same three categories being confused over and over. And the corrections all point at the same deciding factor — the ride status — which is exactly the thing the write-up has to spell out each time.

tagged False Trip → should be Cancellation Fee ×4
tagged False Trip → should be Fare Review ×2
tagged Fare Review → should be False Trip
tagged Cancellation Fee → should be False Trip
tagged Driver Canceled… → should be False Trip

A one-page decision rule — if the ride status is X, file Y — would answer nearly every one of these.

Gap 02 — no "check first" step

Fifteen duplicate tickets in one week

Duplicates are the largest single sub-category, and reviewers keep writing the same instruction back. That repetition is the tell: operators aren't being careless, they were never given the step.

"Please check for an existing complaint before submitting a new NL."
"Please check carefully for an existing NL before submitting a new one."
"Please carefully check the account to confirm whether an NL has been filed."
Gap 03 — taught the fee, not the surprise

Surge pricing and wait fees

The registration deck is thorough on the $0.27 per-minute concierge fee and walks through it word for word. It does not cover surge pricing or multi-stop wait fees — and both show up in the reports as customers hit with a bill they weren't warned about.

"The operator failed to inform the customer that the fare was affected by a
surge factor, resulting in a higher fare than expected." — filed twice, two operators

"OP failed to educate the customer who requested a multi-stop trip, which
resulted in a Wait Fee Tip to Driver of $12.35 being applied."

The registration deck is not the problem

It is genuinely thorough. It is simply aimed at the part of the job that generates the fewest write-ups.

Covered, and covered well

  • Full call structure, intro through confirmation
  • Concierge fee, disclosed word for word
  • Plan pricing with the dollar savings worked out
  • Confirming addresses back to the customer
  • Mobility and accessibility questions
  • The $1 pre-authorization hold
  • Two recorded calls to listen to

Not in anything I could see

  • Which Need Love category to file, and why
  • Checking for an existing ticket first
  • Surge pricing
  • Multi-stop wait fees
  • What ride status means for the ticket

Registration errors account for 5 of 99 write-ups. The training weight and the error weight are pointed in opposite directions.

The quiz score does not predict the mistake

Of the August class members who appear in last week's reports, the person who scored highest on the Need Love module — 140 out of 150 — still filed a ticket under the wrong category. Two others who passed did the same.

That points at something more useful than a low score: the quiz is testing whether someone can recall the categories, not whether they can pick the right one while a customer is on the line. Those are different skills, and only one of them shows up in a write-up.

What I could not see

  • Only four training decks. New material is being written that I don't have access to — a Need Love module may already exist, or be in progress.
  • One week of reports. 99 is enough to show a pattern, not enough to call it a trend. A full quarter would settle it.
  • Nine people overlap. Only nine August-class members appear in last week's reports — far too few to link an individual's quiz score to an individual's error. The class-level pattern is the finding; the person-level one is not.
  • Quiz columns were read off a PDF. The module order comes from the sheet header. Worth a glance to confirm Need Love is the ninth column.
  • No call recordings yet. This is built on what reviewers wrote, not on what was said. The transcripts exist and would sharpen all of it.

What I would do first

  1. Write the Need Love decision rule as one page.If the ride status is X, file Y. Nine of the wrong-category reports name the ride status as the deciding factor.
  2. Add a "check for an existing ticket" step before filing.Fifteen duplicates in one week, and the same correction written back every time.
  3. Put surge and wait fees into the registration script.The deck already handles the concierge fee beautifully. These two just need the same treatment.
  4. Make the Need Love quiz practical, not recall-based.Give them five real tickets to categorize. The top scorer still got it wrong live.
  5. Track NL error rate per operator, per month.Every report carries the operator name, the team lead, and a CallLog ID. It can be counted automatically.
